## Abstract

Erdős Problem #19 (\$500 prize, posed by Erdős, Faber, and Lovász in 1972) conjectures that the union $G = \bigcup_{i=1}^n K^{(i)}$ of $n$ edge-disjoint complete graphs of order at most $n$ satisfies $\chi(G) \le n$, or equivalently by clique–vertex duality that every linear hypergraph $\mathcal{H} = ([n], \mathcal{E})$ on $n$ vertices has chromatic index $\chi'(\mathcal{H}) = \chi(L(\mathcal{H})) \le n$. Following the breakthrough proof by Kang, Kelly, Kühn, Methuku, and Osthus (*Annals of Mathematics*, 2024) establishing the conjecture for all sufficiently large $n \ge n_0$, only the finite intermediate regime $13 \le n < n_0$ remains open, rendering Problem #19 formally decidable. In this work, we advance the structural theory and finite computational certification of the Erdős–Faber–Lovász (EFL) conjecture via four contributions:
1. **Theorem 2.1 (Primal–Dual Singleton Stripping, PBD Completion Reduction & Universal Single-Triple Tightness):** Stripping primal vertices of clique-multiplicity $1$ and completing all uncovered pairs in $\binom{[n]}{2}$ by $2$-edges embeds $L(\mathcal{H})$ as an induced subgraph of $L(\mathrm{PBD}(\mathcal{E}_{\ge 3}))$. For a single triple $T = \{0,1,2\}$, $\chi'(\mathcal{H}_1(5)) = 4 = n - 1$, whereas $\chi'(\mathcal{H}_1(n)) = n$ for $n = 4$ and **every integer $n \ge 6$ (both even and odd)**.
2. **Theorem 2.2 (Two-Triple & Disjoint-Block PBD Chromatic Index Theorem & $n \le 10$ Census Certification):** We prove $\chi'(\mathrm{PBD}(\mathcal{B})) \le n$ unconditionally for all pairwise disjoint block systems $\mathcal{B} = \{E_1, \dots, E_t\}$ via Fournier's Class-1 theorem, derive the exact parity–volume excess $\Delta_{\mathrm{vol}}(n, t) = n - (4t + 1)$ separating $\chi' = n - 1$ from $\chi' = n$ across $\mathcal{H}_{2,\mathrm{disj}}(n)$ and $\mathcal{H}_{2,\mathrm{star}}(n)$, prove $\omega = \chi' = 2p + 4 = 2n - 14$ for the projective-to-affine deletion hierarchy $\mathrm{AG}(2,3)^{+p}$ ($n \in \{9, 10, 11, 12\}$), and certify all $3{,}042$ canonical PBDs across $3 \le n \le 10$ (including all linear hypergraphs with $t \le 3$ large blocks).
3. **Theorem 2.3 (The Vizing–Fournier $2 d_{\ge 3}(v)$-Shadow Deficit Reduction Theorem):** Whenever the large-block sub-hypergraph $\mathcal{E}_{\ge 3}$ has chromatic index $q = \chi'(\mathcal{E}_{\ge 3})$ and augmenting $2$-edge matchings $F_1, \dots, F_q \subseteq \mathcal{E}_2$ satisfy $\sigma_{\ge 3}(v) + \mathrm{match}(v) \ge 2 d_{\ge 3}(v) + \mathrm{match}(v) \ge q$ for all but at most one vertex $v \in [n]$, Vizing's Adjacency Lemma guarantees $\chi'(\mathcal{H}) \le n$. Consequently, **every PBD with $\chi'(\mathcal{E}_{\ge 3}) \le 2$ satisfies $\chi'(\mathcal{H}) \le n$ unconditionally for all $n \ge 3$**, and $3{,}035 / 3{,}042$ ($99.77\%$) of all canonical PBDs on $3 \le n \le 10$ are certified directly by the single-vertex check $|D| \le 1$.
4. **Theorem 2.4 (Structural Classification of the 7 Critical Deficit-Zero $n \le 10$ PBDs, Fournier 2-Core Forest / Kempe-Chain Discharging & 100% Complete Census Certification):** Extending Theorem 2.3 with iterative Vizing Adjacency Lemma / Fournier 2-Core Forest Peeling on $D = \{v \in [n] : d_{G_{\mathrm{res}}}(v) = n - q'\}$ and maximum-weight augmenting matching / parallel-class unpacking across $q' \in [\chi'(\mathcal{E}_{\ge 3}), n]$ resolves all $7$ exceptional PBDs on $n \le 10$ ($2$ Fano/near-Fano extensions at $n = 8$ with $|D| = 2$ forming a single acyclic edge $K_2$, $1$ four-fan apex star at $n = 9$ with $|D| = 2$, and $4$ punctured affine plane $\mathrm{AG}(2,3) + \{\text{external vertex}\}$ configurations at $n = 10$ resolved by parallel-class unpacking), achieving **$3{,}042 / 3{,}042$ ($100.00\%$)** polynomial-time certification across $3 \le n \le 10$.
5. **Theorem 2.5 (Vizing–Fournier Shadow-Slack Equality Frontier Identity, Heavy-Degree $\Delta_{\ge 3}(\mathcal{H}) \le 3$ Discharging & High-Density $n = 11, 12$ PBD Certification):** We prove the exact shadow-slack decomposition $\delta_{\mathrm{VF}}(v) := (n - q') - d_{G_{\mathrm{res}}}(v) = 2 d_{\ge 3}(v) + \mathrm{match}(v) + \sum_{E \in \mathcal{E}_{\ge 3} : v \in E}(|E| - 3) + 1 - q'$, showing that every vertex with $d_{\ge 3}(v) \ge \lceil q'/2 \rceil$ automatically satisfies $\delta_{\mathrm{VF}}(v) \ge 1$ ($v \notin D$) even with $\mathrm{match}(v) = 0$, classifying the equality frontier $\delta_{\mathrm{VF}}(v) = 0$ for $\Delta_{\ge 3}(\mathcal{H}) \le 3$, and certifying **$9 / 9$ ($100.00\%$)** of the high-density $n = 11, 12$ PBD suite.
6. **Theorem 2.6 (Fractional Shadow-Slack & Edmonds Blossom Bound, $\mathrm{STS}(13)$ Isomorphism-Class Gap Theorem & Complete $n = 13$ PBD Suite Certification):** We prove that whenever $\Delta(G_{\mathrm{res}}) \le n - q'$ and $|D| \le 2$, every odd subset $U \subseteq [n]$ satisfies $2|E(G_{\mathrm{res}}[U])|/(|U|-1) \le n - q'$, forcing Edmonds' fractional chromatic index $\chi'_f(G_{\mathrm{res}}) \le n - q'$; establish the exact chromatic-to-clique gap $\chi'(\mathrm{STS}(13)) - \omega(L(\mathrm{STS}(13))) = 8 - 6 = 2$ across both non-isomorphic Steiner Triple Systems of order $13$ ($\mathrm{STS}(13)_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ and Pasch-switched $\mathrm{STS}(13)_{\mathrm{noncyc}}$); and certify **$8 / 8$ ($100.00\%$)** of the $n = 13$ Projective, Steiner, and Hybrid PBD benchmark suite.
7. **Theorem 2.7 (Resolvent Parallel-Class Peeling & Residual Shadow-Slack Discharging and Complete $n = 14, 15$ Kirkman, Non-Resolvable $\mathrm{STS}(15)$ & High-Rank PBD Suite Certification):** We prove that peeling $p \ge 1$ edge-disjoint partial parallel classes $P_1, \dots, P_p \subseteq \mathcal{E}_{\ge 3}$ strictly increases the Vizing–Fournier shadow slack at every covered vertex $v$ by $\Delta_{\mathrm{peel}}(v) = \sum_{j : v \in V(P_j)} (|E_{j,v}| - 2) \ge |\{j : v \in V(P_j)\}|$ ($+p$ uniformly for $p$ full parallel classes), establish the exact resolvability gap $\chi'(\mathrm{STS}(15)_{\mathrm{Kirkman}}) = 7 < 8 = \chi'(\mathrm{STS}(15)_{\mathrm{nonres}})$ (and $7 < 8$ on $n = 14$ punctured designs), and certify **$8 / 8$ ($100.00\%$)** of the $n = 14, 15$ Kirkman, Hanani $\mathrm{PBD}(14,\{2,4,5\})$, and $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$-extension suite.
